I'm a security engineer working on threat intelligence platforms. My work is the platform layer that makes CTI operational: the pipelines that ingest intelligence, the data models that let tools compose, and the connectors and automation that keep a knowledge base current without anyone babysitting it.

Right now I'm building a threat intelligence platform and threat research program, and working on vendor-neutral ways to represent and exchange adversarial-AI (prompt-attack) intelligence.

I write here about the engineering decisions behind that work: why a design was chosen, what it traded away, and where it breaks. I care more about decisions I can defend than results that sound impressive.
